## FAQ

**Q: why do they call them 18 wheelers**

They are called “18 wheelers” because the standard tractor-trailer combination typically has 18 wheels in total. This usually includes 10 wheels on the tractor and 8 wheels on the trailer, though the exact setup can vary depending on the truck configuration, number of axles, and load requirements. The term is a common industry and public nickname used in North America to refer to large freight trucks used for long-haul transportation. It does not mean every truck always has exactly 18 wheels, but it generally describes the typical heavy-duty semi-truck and trailer combination used for commercial shipping.

**Q: how large is a box truck**

A box truck, also called a cube truck or straight truck, typically ranges from about 10 to 26 feet in box length, depending on the application. Smaller box trucks are often around 10–12 feet and used for local deliveries, while medium and larger models can be 14–26 feet for moving, logistics, and freight transport. Overall vehicle size usually depends on the chassis and load capacity, with gross vehicle weight ratings commonly from about 10,000 to 26,000 lbs.
