## Field Notes

Many first-time importers focus on the chassis price and ignore the operating economics that shape real profitability. This is where refrigerated truck maintenance becomes a commercial issue, not just a workshop task. A cheaper unit can become expensive if the compressor is undersized, the insulation is thin, or the body construction leaks air at doors and corners. Buyers also often overlook the weight penalty of thicker insulation versus payload capacity, which can reduce revenue per trip.

Another frequent mistake is buying the wrong power configuration for local conditions. In some markets, engine-driven systems are preferred for simplicity, while others need standby electric options for depots and cold rooms. Importers should also ask about service access, spare parts lead times, and regional support for the reefer unit. If the truck will be used in right-hand-drive countries, RHD refrigerated truck maintenance should be planned from day one, including local workshop familiarity with the chassis layout, wiring, and door hardware.
